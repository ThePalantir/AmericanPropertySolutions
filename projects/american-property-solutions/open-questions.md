## Confirmed Facts

- APS has a public website with broad property maintenance service content.
- APS publicly lists phone, locations, hours, service areas, testimonials, and trust badge imagery.
- APS publicly references commercial properties and four-season property maintenance.
- Human approval is required before final public copy, final sitemap, production deployment, DNS changes, analytics account changes, or public claims about credentials, guarantees, awards, reviews, testimonials, or years of experience.

## Observations

- The current site contains enough information to create a strong pilot direction, but not enough to safely finalize claims.
- The biggest unresolved choice is whether APS should be positioned as commercial-first or equally residential/commercial.
- The current service-area language is broad and inconsistent.
- Trust signals may be valuable but need confirmation before reuse.

## Assumptions

- For the pilot concept, it is acceptable to use publicly listed facts while clearly marking claims that need approval.
- The first demo should prioritize quote requests and phone calls over careers, partner recruiting, or long company history.
- Service pages should be grouped before detailed copy is written.

## Recommendations

- Resolve the high-priority questions before final sitemap and final public copy.
- Use the pilot demo to show direction, not to imply every claim is approved.
- Ask the client for a short validation pass rather than a long discovery process.
- Treat trust signals as an approval checklist.
